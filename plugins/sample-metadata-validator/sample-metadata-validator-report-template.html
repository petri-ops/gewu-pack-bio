This report is generated by the Petri sample-metadata-validator tool at runtime. It is the upstream design gate: before expression QC or differential analysis runs, it verifies that the counts matrix and the sample metadata can actually support the requested design and contrast — sample alignment, duplicates, group completeness, replicate numbers and confounded designs are all checked here.

What This Gate Validates

  • Sample alignment — every counts column is matched against the metadata sample IDs; unmatched samples are reported per side in sample_alignment.csv (counts-only vs metadata-only).
  • Duplicates — duplicated sample IDs in either the counts matrix or the metadata are flagged.
  • Design column & contrast levels — the configured design column must exist, and both the reference and treatment groups must be present in the data.
  • Replicates — each contrast group is compared against min_replicates; a group below the minimum blocks modeling.
  • Confounded design — a design where all samples fall into a single group, or where the contrast cannot be estimated, is rejected before any modeling is attempted.
  • Modeling readiness — the final verdict (can model in the summary above) requires ≥ 2 samples per contrast group, ≥ 2 distinct groups, and no blocking failure.

How to Interpret This Report

  • VALID — the design supports the requested contrast. The aligned metadata (validated_metadata.csv) can be passed to expression-qc and then deseq2.
  • WARNING — validation completed with recorded caveats (for example some samples dropped during alignment). Review the warnings; a warning here usually means a subset of your data will not take part in the contrast.
  • BLOCKED — the design cannot support the contrast (missing columns, duplicated IDs, zero overlap, missing contrast levels, insufficient replicates, or a fully confounded design). Do not proceed to modeling until the design or data is fixed.

Methods Summary

Structural validation only — no expression statistics are computed. Sample IDs are intersected between the counts matrix columns and the metadata rows; the design column is checked for the presence of both contrast levels; per-group sample counts are compared with min_replicates; and single-group or non-estimable designs are rejected as confounded. Alignment and design facts are written out unchanged for downstream reproducibility.

Caveats: (1) min_replicates is a scientific decision, not a universal constant — lowering it to unblock a run changes the statistical power of everything downstream; (2) this gate checks design feasibility, not data quality — run expression-qc next; (3) samples present on only one side are reported, never silently merged or dropped by name similarity.
